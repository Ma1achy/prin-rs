Read `coll` and `dead` before anything else: a rho over collapsed or one-sided quads
is a correlation between two things that are not being measured.

Then read `rho bnd`, never `rho all`. The all-quads column is dominated by tame quads
where both arms read near zero and agree trivially; it is high by construction and
says nothing about the population the budget is spent on.

`scale` is between_matched/within at EQUAL sample count, so it isolates cell-extent
against quad-extent. `count` is within_pooled/between at EQUAL extent, so it isolates
the small-sample bias. Neither is meaningful without the other: comparing the raw
within and between numbers moves both at once, and a spread estimator's expectation
depends on its sample count (E+1=2 reports 0.539 of E+1=32's value in near-field).

A rho over fewer than ~20 quads is a draw, not a measurement. Read `n mix`/`n bnd`
beside every stratified rho and discard the ones with no population -- `med hot` says
why: where it sits at 1.000 the quad is SATURATED, every footprint hot, no internal
edge and correctly not a boundary. That is a real reading, not a failure of the mask.

If `rho mix` is high and `scale` is near 1, the two arms are the same statistic at two
extents and §1's mechanism is wrong -- the fault is the aggregation, and the fix is
§3.1/§3.2 rather than a new arm. If `rho mix` is low, §1 is right and this note is
wrong.
