the surrogate and not the estimator, and the remedy is to render twice rather than
to calibrate a correction.

The true-parent column is FLAT in E under Halton at 0.0227, while the pooled one
runs 0.67 -> 0.07. The pooled bias is the surrogate, not the estimator, and it is
removed by rendering rather than by calibrating. The residual 0.0227 is what a
true two-resolution comparison actually costs, and it does not shrink with E.

Under PCG the true parent is WORSE at small E (0.1157 against 0.2798 pooled is
better, but 0.0553 against 0.0762 at E+1=8 is a much smaller gap): pooling gives
4x the samples, and PCG's per-footprint randomisation partly compensates for the
surrogate error it would otherwise have. Two wrongs, partially cancelling.
