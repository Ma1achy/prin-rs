HOW TO READ THIS

**If `at a cell boundary` concentrates near 1 while the null does not, the wedges are
chart-selection artefacts** -- the representation changing where nothing in the dynamics
does -- and Heggie's global regularisation is the principled cure, because it removes the
argmax rather than smoothing it.

If the two populations agree, the itinerary differences are NOT tie crossings: they are
neighbouring trajectories that have genuinely diverged by that boundary, and the cells
are a chaos structure the reference choice merely reports. **The first-divergence
boundary index decides which reading is available**: early divergence across the frame
means ties; late means chaos had time to separate them first.
