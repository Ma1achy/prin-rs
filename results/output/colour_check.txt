Cell width halves every level, so a field proportional to it would show `ratio`
= 2.000 in every row. Measured 1.19-1.62, FALLING with depth: the dependence is
sub-linear and saturates, which is the chaotic-divergence signature -- past some
cell width the copies decohere fully however close they started. Consistent with
the standing measurement that cell width falling 9x moves sigma_E(0) by 8.6x
(proportional) and `ensemble_spread` by only 2.1x.

So a bivariate render DOES carry a mild scale term: a level-0 leaf reads brighter
than a level-4 leaf at the same place. Under the log ramp that is about 12% of the
lightness range across five levels. Stated rather than corrected -- normalising by
cell width would change what the field means. `t_end` is the scale-free control and
is flat, which is what says the effect above is the cell width and not the depth.

Read `distinct` and `modal%` before either image. A field whose modal value covers a
large fraction of the region has that many pixels painted one colour, and the picture
is describing the estimator rather than the physics. `event%` says whether the event
arm is what is doing it -- it is a count ratio over E+1 copies and is quantised by
construction, so where it dominates, `spread_shape` is the field with structure in it.

`n0 span` is the extent of the shape-sphere axis the previous hue map discarded. It is
the size of what was invisible, measured per region rather than asserted -- but it
is a MAX statistic, so read `n0 interdecile` beside it. A span of 2 with a tiny
interdecile means the axis was reached by a few pixels, not used by the region.

`hue coverage` chooses kappa on a number. A region that genuinely visits one kind
of configuration SHOULD read near zero at every kappa, and that is a true answer
rather than a broken palette -- `far` is the control for exactly that.
