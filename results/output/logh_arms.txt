**Read the stepper-only control first.** `plain_rk4` against `plain_lf` at equal
evaluations is the stepper's own contribution. If they differ substantially, that
amount is inside every other comparison in the table; if they agree, the
regularisation comparison is clean.

**Then `logh_lf` against `heggie`.** That is the falsification test. logH has no chart
and therefore no re-registration at all, which is a stronger form of the property
Heggie's win is attributed to. If it loses, the attribution is wrong or incomplete and
the next candidates are the KS square-root's own round-off and the per-boundary energy
re-freeze -- a real fork that deserves its own run, not a paragraph.

**`far` is prediction 2.** AZ wins it outright today, on all 65536 pixels by a flat
0.7-0.9 decades, and that win is attributed to AZ never re-registering there. A method
that never re-registers anywhere should not lose it.

**`logh_rk4` against `logh_lf` is prediction 4.** On shell `K + B == U`, so an
integrator that evaluates both denominators at the same point sees only a Sundman
transformation; the leapfrog sees them at different points and that asymmetry is the
method. If the two arms come out alike, that prediction is wrong and so is Mikkola &
Merritt's sentence it rests on.
